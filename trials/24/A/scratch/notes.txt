General
==================================================================
Where I hesitated in the grammar
- "Two beats standing, then the jumps": since a jump is written at its LANDING and its
  flight is in the word before, "stand 2 beats, then jump" can mean the first jump lands
  on 2 (takes off ~1.3) or on 3. The grammar's rule is clear, but a task in plain words is
  not; nothing in the grammar shows the "stand N beats and then jump" idiom.
- What `up(Ncm)` measures: "how high the body rises". The task says "feet 6 cm off the
  floor". They turned out equal (the feet stay level), but the grammar could say so:
  "the feet leave the floor by the same height".
- Where a jump from ONE foot onto both (`jump`, `jump.together`) lands: not said. It landed
  halfway toward the foot that was held up (6 cm back).
- `together` + a weight already on one foot at beat 0: no way to say both as first words.
Things I could not say
- A jump from both feet onto one foot (sissonne / rope skipping from two feet to one):
  hop/leap from both is SH2012, so I needed a `shift` beat in between.
- 4 cm / 6 cm jumps every half beat, 12 cm hop every beat at 120: the model's minimum
  times forbid them. Only SH2016 tells you; the grammar's numbers (5 cm 0.34 s, 10 cm
  0.49 s, "0.25 s gives 2 cm") let me predict it, but a table "at tempo T, one jump every
  N beats can reach H cm" would help a game-animation writer.
Grammar improvement proposals
1. In "A jump", add the idiom: "stand for N beats and then jump: the jump word is written
   where it lands; with a written height the body stands until (height time) before it.
   `together:2 jump.up(6cm)` takes off at about beat 1.3 and lands on 2."
2. Give the height-time table up to 30 cm, and the max height for 0.25 s (2.7 cm written)
   and 0.5 s (10.6 cm): "the most a written height can get in the time".
3. Say where `jump` from one foot lands (between the feet? beside the standing foot?).
4. Say that `ends ... of where it began` measures the middle of both feet, including one
   held in the air, or change it to the standing foot when one foot is up.
5. Say that `q` may follow a bar line and repeat a group written in an earlier bar, and
   that `%2` may repeat a bar made of a `q` (both worked; I was not sure).
6. A cheat line for "jump from two feet onto one" (even if the answer is "shift first,
   which costs a word").
What the sheet showed / did not show
- Shown well: the coupe behind/in front and the crossing (front view), the held leg
  behind during hops, bends before jumps (side view, closer --step), and travel on the
  floor plan.
- Not shown: jump heights in cm (2-6 cm are a few pixels); half-beat jumps on a
  half-beat sheet look like standing; whether a small hop (2.4 cm) leaves the floor.
